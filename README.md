**Intelligibility is relation.**

Relation constrains what can follow.
That constraint is agency.
When the relation becomes sufficient, it completes.
When that completed relation can be reconstructed through another carrier, it persists.

**Communication is the inheritance of completed relational structure across carriers.**

Meaning may be unlimited.
Its transmissible forms need not be.

**Relational intelligibility is factorizable.**

A sentence is not defined by punctuation. It is defined by completed intelligibility.

Punctuation can mark completion, but it cannot cause it. A full stop can appear where intelligibility has not completed, and intelligibility can complete where no full stop appears.

What makes a sentence a sentence is therefore not the carrier alone, but the relational organization that becomes sufficient to stand as a whole.

A theorem is a sentence.

Its wording, notation, language, speaker, script, and physical medium may all change while the theorem remains intelligibly recoverable.

Therefore the identity of an intelligible structure is not identical with the identity of its carrier.

What persists across carriers is relational organization.

A carrier succeeds when another intelligence can reconstruct that organization sufficiently to inherit it.

Communication is therefore not the transfer of a substance called meaning. It is the reconstruction of intelligible relational structure across a change of carrier.

For such reconstruction to occur, at least three things must be distinguishable:

What changes.

What makes the change sufficient.

What remains available after completion.

These are not three kinds of meaning. They are three functions required for transmissible intelligibility.

Factorization begins here.
