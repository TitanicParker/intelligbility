# The Third Derivation: The Recurrent Values of the Factors

## Where pure derivation ends and recovered structure begins

The previous documents established that transmissible intelligibility is factorable into at least three non-redundant functions:

- what changes;
- what makes the change sufficient;
- how the achieved result remains available.

That result does **not** by itself determine how many recurrent values each factor has.

This document therefore marks an important transition.

We are no longer deriving the number of values from logic alone.

We are asking what recurrent answers have been recovered when the three questions are applied repeatedly.

The current grammar stabilizes around:

- six recurrent Transformation Patterns;
- three recurrent Completion Topologies;
- three recurrent Persistence Modes.

The goal here is to make those values intelligible in plain language and to show why they are genuinely different from one another.

---

# Part I — Transformation Pattern

Transformation Pattern answers:

> **What kind of unresolvedness changes?**

The six current recurrent answers are:

1. Differentiation
2. Opening
3. Relating
4. Repair
5. Integration
6. Settlement

These are not six topics.

They are six different ways in which an unresolved relation can become otherwise.

---

## 1. Differentiation

Sometimes the problem is that a difference does not yet count.

Two things are conflated.

A relevant boundary is missing.

A candidate has not been singled out.

A contrast is present but not operative.

Completion occurs when a difference becomes determinate enough to matter.

That is Differentiation.

Plainly:

> **Before: the required difference is not operative.  
> After: the difference is operative.**

Examples can include identifying which wire is live, distinguishing two diagnoses, marking a boundary, separating two concepts, or recognizing that two apparently similar cases must be treated differently.

The essential change is not motion, repair, or choice.

It is the acquisition of operative difference.

---

## 2. Opening

Sometimes the problem is restriction.

A route is blocked.

An option is unavailable.

A field cannot yet be reached.

A continuation is closed.

Completion occurs when availability increases.

That is Opening.

Plainly:

> **Before: something relevant is unavailable.  
> After: it becomes available.**

Opening is not simply beginning.

A process can begin without opening anything.

Opening is specifically the transformation from restriction to increased reach, access, continuation, option, or availability.

---

## 3. Relating

Sometimes the elements are present but do not yet bear on one another in the required way.

A dependency has not been established.

A correspondence is missing.

A sequence has not become ordered.

Two terms exist but their bearing is not constitutive.

Completion occurs when a relation becomes part of what the situation is.

That is Relating.

Plainly:

> **Before: the required bearing is absent or insufficient.  
> After: the bearing becomes constitutive.**

Relating is different from merely having more things present.

The change is in how the terms matter to one another.

---

## 4. Repair

Sometimes the situation is already organized, but the organization fails relative to an operative criterion.

A mechanism is malfunctioning.

A calculation contains an error.

A procedure violates its own requirement.

A mixture lies outside the required range.

Completion occurs when the governing misfit no longer controls the result.

That is Repair.

Plainly:

> **Before: a fault governs the organization.  
> After: the fault no longer governs it.**

Repair does not require moral judgment.

The relevant criterion can be mechanical, logical, mathematical, procedural, practical, or local to the system.

---

## 5. Integration

Sometimes the parts exist but do not yet form a working whole.

They may be adjacent without being organized.

They may be collected without having roles.

They may coexist without mutual support.

Completion occurs when the parts acquire determinate roles through a whole.

That is Integration.

Plainly:

> **Before: there is unorganized plurality.  
> After: the parts participate in an organized whole.**

Integration is therefore not mere aggregation.

A heap is not integrated simply because many things are together.

The defining change is role-bearing organization.

---

## 6. Settlement

Sometimes the problem is not missing difference, blocked access, missing bearing, fault, or lack of integration.

The problem is that a demand remains live.

A choice still has to be made.

A dispute still has to be decided.

A journey still has to be completed.

An explanation still has to answer something.

An obligation still remains open.

Completion occurs when the demand for further uptake is discharged.

That is Settlement.

Plainly:

> **Before: something still has to be done, decided, explained, or continued.  
> After: that demand no longer governs the field.**

Settlement is not merely stopping.

A process can stop accidentally while remaining unresolved.

Settlement is the discharge of residual demand.

---

## The six as forms of unresolvedness

The six Transformation Patterns can be remembered through six opening conditions:

- no operative difference → Differentiation;
- restricted availability → Opening;
- missing constitutive bearing → Relating;
- governed misfit → Repair;
- unorganized plurality → Integration;
- residual demand → Settlement.

This does not yet prove that no seventh transformation could ever be discovered.

It does show that the current six are not arbitrary labels.

Each answers a distinct kind of unresolvedness.

---

# Part II — Completion Topology

Completion Topology answers:

> **What structural condition makes the transformation enough?**

The current grammar recovers three recurrent answers:

1. Focal
2. Distributed
3. Release

These should not be understood primarily as spatial directions.

They are forms of sufficiency.

---

## 7. Focal

Sometimes completion depends on a privileged locus.

One item is selected.

One connection is repaired.

One source is established.

One endpoint is reached.

One whole becomes the relevant organizing centre.

One decision becomes settled.

Completion gathers around that locus.

That is Focal completion.

Plainly:

> **This is enough because this locus now carries the decisive completion.**

Focal does not mean physically central.

It means structurally privileged.

---

## 8. Distributed

Sometimes no single locus is enough.

Completion requires a span, plurality, sequence, field, reciprocity, or system.

A rule must hold across cases.

A network must become connected across nodes.

A sequence must be ordered across stages.

A team must function through several roles.

A relation must hold across more than one position.

That is Distributed completion.

Plainly:

> **This is enough only because the relevant plurality is jointly organized.**

The defining feature is not simply that many things are present.

It is that plurality belongs to the condition of completion itself.

---

## 9. Release

Sometimes completion occurs because something that previously governed the field loses its hold.

A conflation is broken.

A restriction ceases to govern.

A fault is removed.

An imbalance stops determining the system.

A compulsory continuation is discharged.

An old interpretation no longer controls the whole.

That is Release.

Plainly:

> **This is enough because what had been governing the unresolved state no longer governs it.**

Release is not merely movement away from something.

It is de-governance.

That is why it belongs beside Focal and Distributed as a condition of sufficiency rather than as ordinary direction.

---

## The three as answers to “why is it enough?”

The three topologies can be compressed to:

- **Focal** — enough through a privileged locus;
- **Distributed** — enough through a constitutive plurality;
- **Release** — enough through loss of prior governance.

Again, this is the current recurrent set.

The important achievement is that each value states a different reason why the transformation counts as complete.

---

# Part III — Persistence Mode

Persistence Mode answers:

> **In what mode does the achieved completion remain available?**

The current grammar recovers three recurrent answers:

1. Standing
2. Bearing
3. Efficacy

These are not three substances.

They are three modes of post-completion availability.

---

## 10. Standing

Sometimes what remains available is the achieved determination itself.

The selected item stands.

The established distinction stands.

The open route stands.

The repaired arrangement stands.

The settled decision stands.

The result can be taken up directly as what has now been achieved.

That is Standing.

Plainly:

> **There it is.**

Standing does not require physical stillness.

A standing relation can remain dynamic.

A standing route can be used.

The point is that the achieved determination itself is available for uptake.

---

## 11. Bearing

Sometimes the result remains what it is only through a constitutive relation.

A choice remains a choice against alternatives.

A proportion remains through the quantities related.

A rule remains attached to a condition.

A correction remains valid within a specified range.

A result is recoverable through a source, contrast, support, correspondence, frame, dependency, or reciprocity.

That is Bearing.

Plainly:

> **It holds through this relation.**

If the bearing is removed, the result is no longer the same completed form.

---

## 12. Efficacy

Sometimes the completion remains primarily as an accomplished consequence or capacity.

The device now works.

The route can now be travelled.

The boundary now prevents passage.

The rule now governs action.

The team can now perform.

The obligation has now been discharged.

That is Efficacy.

Plainly:

> **Now it works, acts, governs, permits, prevents, or has been accomplished.**

Efficacy need not mean continuous activity.

A dormant capacity can still be efficacious.

The point is that the completion has changed what can happen.

---

## The three as modes of continued availability

The persistence triplet can be compressed to:

- **Standing** — the achieved determination itself remains available;
- **Bearing** — the result remains through a constitutive relation;
- **Efficacy** — the result remains as accomplished consequence or capacity.

These are different answers to the same post-completion question.

---

# Part IV — Why these are factor values rather than a list

The important thing is not merely that there are six names, three names, and three names.

Each set belongs to a different functional slot.

A transformation answers **what changed**.

A topology answers **why that change is enough**.

A persistence mode answers **how the achieved result remains available**.

That is why the same value on one factor can recur across many values of another.

Repair can complete focally, distributively, or by release.

A focal completion can occur through Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, or Settlement.

Efficacy can remain after many different transformations and under many different completion conditions.

The values therefore behave as reusable contributions rather than as 54 unrelated labels.

---

# Part V — What has and has not been established

At this point we can responsibly say:

> The current recovered grammar uses six recurrent answers to the transformation question, three recurrent answers to the sufficiency question, and three recurrent answers to the persistence question.

We should not silently turn that into stronger claims.

This document does **not** establish:

- that six is metaphysically necessary;
- that no seventh transformation could ever be discovered;
- that three topologies are the only logically possible topologies;
- that three persistence modes exhaust every conceivable ontology;
- that the factors are unique;
- that every event in every domain must fit the system.

Those are different claims.

What has been achieved is enough for the next step.

We now have a factorized grammar with:

\[
6 \text{ transformation values}
\]

\[
3 \text{ completion values}
\]

\[
3 \text{ persistence values}
\]

The next question is:

> **What follows when these reusable values are composed?**
