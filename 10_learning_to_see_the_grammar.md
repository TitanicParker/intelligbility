# Learning to See the Grammar

## A first curriculum in completed intelligibility

The purpose of this curriculum is not to make the learner memorize fifty-four cell names.

The purpose is to change what the learner can notice.

The grammar should eventually become as immediate as hearing that a melody has resolved, seeing that a bridge is complete, recognizing that an argument has landed, or knowing that a debt has been discharged.

The learner begins with ordinary intelligibility.

The terminology comes later.

---

## Lesson 1 — Feel completion before naming it

Read these:

> The red one.

> There. That one.

> I mean this mark, not the others.

What happened?

Do not classify yet.

Notice that an initially insufficient field has become determinate enough for one thing to matter as distinguished.

Now read:

> We have decided.

> That is the one we are choosing.

> The question is settled: this option.

Again, something lands.

But it is not the same landing.

In the first group, a difference becomes operative.

In the second, a live demand for choice is discharged.

The first lesson is therefore simple:

> **Completion has structure.**

Different completed sentences can finish different kinds of unresolvedness.

---

## Lesson 2 — The six ways the field can change

The first factor asks:

> **What changed?**

There are six recurrent Transformation Patterns.

### Differentiation

A difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated, or non-operative becomes determinate enough to matter.

> This one, not that one.

### Opening

Availability, reach, continuation, access, or possibility becomes greater.

> Another way is open.

### Relating

A bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, reciprocity, or mutual determination becomes constitutive.

> This depends on that.

### Repair

A misfit or fault relative to an operative criterion ceases to govern the result.

> It is set right now.

### Integration

Separate parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole.

> They work as one system now.

### Settlement

A live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, adjudication, or continuation is discharged.

> That settles it.

Do not memorize the sample sentences.

They are only doors.

The Transformation Pattern is the kind of unresolvedness that changes.

---

## Lesson 3 — Relation is underneath all six

A common mistake is to think that only Relating is relational.

It is not.

All intelligibility is relational.

Differentiation requires a field in which a difference can matter.

Opening requires a relation between prior and expanded possibility.

Repair requires relation to a criterion.

Integration requires relations among parts.

Settlement requires a governing demand and its discharge.

Relating is special because the relation itself becomes constitutive of what changed.

So remember:

> **Relation is the ground. Relating is a transformation.**

---

## Lesson 4 — What made the change enough?

Knowing what changed is not enough to know the completed form.

Ask a second question:

> **What made that change sufficient?**

Three Completion Topologies recur.

### Focal

Completion gathers around one privileged locus.

> This one.

> The source is here.

> We chose this option.

The privileged locus is not incidental. It is constitutive of why the transformation counts as complete.

### Distributed

Completion requires a plurality, span, sequence, reciprocal field, set of cases, or system.

> It holds across the range.

No single point would be enough.

### Release

Completion occurs because something that previously governed the situation loses determining force.

> That requirement no longer governs us.

Release is not merely negative wording.

It is de-governance.

---

## Lesson 5 — What remains?

Now ask:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Three Persistence Modes recur.

### Standing

The achieved determination itself remains available.

> Chosen.

### Bearing

The achieved result remains what it is through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, frame, support, or mutual determination.

> We are square with each other.

### Efficacy

The achieved result remains as a capacity, effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.

> We can proceed now.

Again, do not confuse the ground with the factor.

Every intelligibility is relational.

Bearing is specifically a way in which the completed result persists.

---

## Lesson 6 — Feel a cell

Now we combine nothing additively.

Instead, we let three constraints mutually determine one event.

Read:

> We’re even.

> Nothing remains due either way.

> We can leave it there between us.

> The matter rests on that understanding now.

What keeps happening?

A live demand is discharged.

It becomes complete because the prior demand loses governance.

The result remains through the relation now holding between the parties.

Only now name it:

> **Settlement × Release × Bearing**

This is one cell.

The coordinate is not three detachable properties.

It is one completed form generated under three simultaneous constraints.

---

## Lesson 7 — Feel the neighbor

Now read:

> We can move on now.

> That issue no longer blocks the next step.

> The process can continue without that demand deciding anything further.

Much is the same.

The Transformation remains Settlement.

The Topology remains Release.

But something changed.

In the previous stack, what survived was the relation established by settlement.

Here, what survives is changed continuation: something can now happen.

The coordinate is:

> **Settlement × Release × Efficacy**

The learner has just encountered a cell border.

That border teaches more than a definition of Bearing or Efficacy could teach alone.

---

## Lesson 8 — Hold Persistence fixed and change Transformation

Bearing does not mean one topic.

Compare:

> This is larger than that.

A distinction persists through comparison.

Now:

> The price depends on the weight.

A relation itself becomes constitutive and persists through dependency.

Now:

> The two pieces are correctly aligned with each other.

A fault has been corrected and the result persists through their proper bearing.

Now:

> Each part supports the others in the structure.

Separate parts have become an organized whole whose achieved unity persists through mutual support.

Now:

> We are square with each other.

A demand has been discharged and the settlement persists through the relation between the parties.

The same Persistence Mode can occur through radically different transformations.

This is why the factors are not synonyms for topics.

---

## Lesson 9 — Hold Transformation fixed and move through Persistence

Take Settlement × Release.

### Standing

> The matter is finished. It remains settled.

The achieved determination stands.

### Bearing

> Nothing remains due between us.

The settlement remains through a relation.

### Efficacy

> We can move on now.

The settlement remains as changed continuation.

The surface differences can be tiny.

The structural differences are not.

Learning the grammar means becoming sensitive to that difference.

---

## Lesson 10 — Hold Transformation and Persistence fixed and change Topology

Suppose the Transformation is Settlement and the Persistence is Standing.

A Focal completion might gather around one decisive choice:

> This is the option.

A Distributed completion might require recurrence or sufficiency across a field:

> Across the cases, the matter is settled.

A Release completion might become sufficient because a previous governor loses force:

> The old demand is over; the settled result stands.

The same broad transformation and persistence can therefore land through different shapes of enoughness.

---

## Lesson 11 — Stop listening for keywords

The grammar cannot be learned as a word-matching system.

“Between” does not automatically mean Bearing.

“No longer” does not automatically mean Release.

“One” does not automatically mean Focal.

“All” does not automatically mean Distributed.

“Works” does not automatically mean Efficacy.

A sentence can use any of those words while performing a different structural event.

Ask about the event:

> What changed?

> What made it enough?

> What remains available?

The words are evidence.

The completed relational movement is the object.

---

## Lesson 12 — Learn by stacking

A useful stack should become more convincing as its examples become less alike.

For one cell, use examples from different domains:

- an ordinary conversation;
- a mathematical situation;
- a legal settlement;
- a mechanical system;
- a story;
- a scientific procedure;
- an institution;
- a map or spatial relation.

If all eight still land in recognizably the same way, the common structure becomes easier to separate from subject matter.

This is the central exercise of the curriculum.

> **Find what survives variation.**

---

## Lesson 13 — Learn by perturbation

Once a cell can be felt, change exactly one dimension.

Do not ask first for the new name.

Ask:

> **What changed in the landing?**

This is controlled perturbation.

It teaches the dimensions through their effects.

The learner should eventually be able to predict that a change from Bearing to Efficacy will alter how the result remains available, while leaving the governing Transformation and shape of enoughness recognizably stable.

At that point the grammar has become generative knowledge.

---

## Lesson 14 — Generate from the coordinate

Recognition is only one direction.

Now reverse it.

Give the learner:

> **Integration × Distributed × Bearing**

Ask for an ordinary situation that could instantiate it.

A good answer must contain one unified event in which separate parts become an organized whole; completion requires a constitutive plurality or system; and the achieved whole remains what it is through mutual support or relation among its parts.

For example:

> A stone arch is complete when the stones take their places across the span and the finished structure remains standing through the way their loads bear through one another.

The learner is no longer merely recognizing examples.

The coordinate constrains imagination.

---

## Lesson 15 — The final recognition

After enough practice, the learner should be able to move in both directions:

> experience → completed form → coordinate

and

> coordinate → constrained form → new experience

When both routes become possible, the fifty-four cells stop looking like a catalogue.

They become a grammar.

And when the learner sees why the grammar exists at all, the grammar stops looking like an arbitrary taxonomy.

It becomes the articulated structure of a philosophy:

> intelligibility is relation;

> relation constrains;

> constraint can become sufficient;

> sufficiency produces completion;

> completion can persist;

> persistent structure can be reconstructed;

> reconstruction makes inheritance possible.

The learner has travelled from philosophy to cell and from cell back to philosophy.

The ring is closed.
