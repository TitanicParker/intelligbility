# Meaning Rides on Structure

## Humans, GPTs, and the reconstruction of intelligibility

A sentence can change its words and remain intelligible in recognizably the same way.

A theorem can change notation.

A story can be translated.

An instruction can become a diagram.

A proof can contract into a proposition and later be reopened.

A spoken agreement can become a written contract.

Across these changes, the carrier varies while something remains reconstructable.

This gives us a direct way into the problem of meaning.

> **Meaning rides on structure.**

The claim is not that wording is irrelevant.

Wording is one of the principal ways structure is carried.

The claim is that intelligibility cannot be identical with a particular wording if recognizably the same intelligible organization can survive replacement of that wording.

The problem of meaning can therefore be approached experimentally through reconstruction.

---

## 1. The GPT problem revealed the method

Large language models made this problem unusually visible.

A GPT is extraordinarily capable of producing plausible semantic continuation.

That is useful.

It is also dangerous for structural investigation.

If the model sees the sentence, the intended coordinate, the canonical cell name, the companion utterance, and the desired conclusion at the same time, agreement proves very little.

The answer can be pulled toward itself by information leakage.

So the research architecture had to become stricter.

One model sees the sentence but not the grammar.

Another sees frozen evidence plus the grammar and recovers a coordinate.

Another sees the coordinate and primitive grammar but not the source sentence or canonical atlas.

Only a coordinator sees the entire circuit.

The model was not given more freedom in order to discover structure.

It was given less.

Blindness, frozen evidence, role separation, independent derivation, negative constraints, and round trips were introduced to prevent unconstrained semantic completion from manufacturing convergence.

This methodological history is part of the theory.

It suggests:

> **The central problem is not the absence of meaning. It is the discipline of meaning by structure.**

---

## 2. The round trip

The strongest circuit runs in two directions.

First:

> sentence → blind account of its movement → recovered coordinate

Then independently:

> coordinate → derived completed form → new ordinary expression

The second route does not see the first sentence.

If the final derived form nevertheless converges structurally with the originating sentence, the exact wording cannot explain the convergence.

Something has survived successive changes of representation.

The experiment therefore asks:

> **What invariant is preserved strongly enough to reconstruct intelligibility at the other end?**

That invariant is a candidate object for the study of meaning.

---

## 3. Meaning as constrained reconstruction

A useful provisional formulation is:

> **Meaning is the reconstruction of relational organization under sufficient constraint.**

This formulation has several advantages.

It does not require meaning to be a substance carried intact from one mind to another.

It does not identify meaning with the physical carrier.

It does not require identical wording.

It makes misunderstanding intelligible: reconstruction can diverge.

It makes ambiguity intelligible: the carrier can permit more than one sufficiently coherent reconstruction.

It makes teaching intelligible: instruction changes what structures a learner can reconstruct.

It makes translation intelligible: a new carrier can preserve enough relational organization for a corresponding reconstruction.

And it makes both humans and GPTs available to experiment without first deciding whether their inner experience is the same.

---

## 4. Human and GPT

A human and a GPT are not the same kind of system.

A human is embodied, biographical, perceptual, affective, social, temporally continuous, and physically situated in ways a text model is not.

But comparison does not require identity.

Both can receive carriers.

Both can distinguish some relations.

Both can reconstruct continuations.

Both can preserve some structure under paraphrase.

Both can misunderstand.

Both can over-complete.

Both can learn discriminations from examples.

That is enough to begin.

The first question need not be:

> Does the GPT really mean?

A more productive question is:

> **What relational organization can humans and GPTs reconstruct across carrier variation, and what constraints determine fidelity or failure?**

---

## 5. Teaching becomes an experiment on meaning

The 54-cell corpus gives us something unusual.

It gives us controlled families of completed intelligibility.

For each coordinate we can assemble semantically distant examples while attempting to preserve one completion profile.

Then we can teach without naming.

Show a stack.

Ask what is common.

Add examples from distant domains.

See whether the proposed commonality survives.

Show a one-axis neighbor.

Ask what changed.

Only later reveal the coordinate.

This can be done with humans.

It can be done with GPTs.

The same basic instrument can therefore study how two very different intelligences acquire sensitivity to the same proposed structural invariants.

---

## 6. The crucial tests

The grammar gives us a family of direct experiments.

### Carrier substitution

Keep the proposed completed structure stable while changing wording, language, domain, modality, or representation.

Does recognition survive?

### Structural perturbation

Keep most of the event stable while changing one dimension of the coordinate.

Does the perceived landing move?

### Blind generation

Give only the coordinate and primitive definitions.

Can a new realization be generated that independent judges recognize as belonging to the same family?

### Blind recovery

Give an unfamiliar realization without its coordinate.

Can the coordinate be recovered?

### Cross-domain transfer

Teach through one set of domains and test in another.

Does the learner recognize the structure rather than the topical costume?

### Cross-language transfer

Teach or resolve in one language and test in another.

What survives lexical and syntactic replacement?

### Neighbor discrimination

Can the learner distinguish two cells differing by one dimension?

### False completion

Can a fluent carrier be recognized as insufficiently licensed when the relational route does not support its landing?

These experiments turn meaning into something that can be varied without pretending it has been reduced to a single number.

---

## 7. The excess of meaning

A GPT often fails in a revealing way.

It does not fail because it cannot produce an interpretation.

It fails because it can produce too many.

It can infer an intention that was never supplied.

It can make a category fit by redescribing the evidence.

It can import a familiar philosophical meaning into a term that has been locally defined more narrowly.

It can produce a beautifully coherent answer whose coherence was not earned by the available relation.

Humans can do versions of the same thing.

We anticipate.

We stereotype.

We normalize unfamiliar claims into familiar categories.

We remember what would have made sense rather than exactly what occurred.

We complete patterns.

Generativity is part of intelligence.

So is the danger of false completion.

The study of meaning must therefore study not only what can be reconstructed, but what constrains reconstruction enough to make it answerable to the carrier and its inherited field.

---

## 8. Meaning and the 54

The fifty-four coordinates are not fifty-four meanings.

A single coordinate can support indefinitely many subjects, sentences, experiences, institutions, stories, and situations.

The proposal is instead that the grammar describes recurrent forms by which relational intelligibility becomes sufficiently complete to support determinate uptake.

Meaning can therefore be semantically abundant while its completed transmissible organization remains structurally finite.

This is not paradoxical.

A finite alphabet supports indefinitely many texts.

A finite set of musical intervals supports indefinitely many melodies.

The analogy is imperfect, because the grammar concerns completion rather than symbol combination.

But the general lesson is useful:

> **Finite form does not imply finite content.**

The 54 may function as recurrent conjugations through which unbounded semantic material becomes completed enough to be inherited.

---

## 9. Meaning becomes teachable because structure recurs

If every meaningful event were structurally unique, teaching meaning would require teaching each event independently.

Recurrence changes that.

Once a learner recognizes a completion form across sufficiently different examples, a new instance can be understood partly through the inherited structural discrimination.

The learner does not merely remember examples.

The learner acquires a constraint.

That constraint changes what can be noticed next.

Teaching therefore participates in the same philosophy as the grammar itself:

> relation constrains continuation.

A learned distinction is a new relation available to perception.

It changes the learner's future space of intelligible uptake.

Teaching is itself an act of structural agency.

---

## 10. A research programme

The immediate programme can now be stated without resolving the philosophy of mind in advance.

Build matched human and GPT learning tasks from the same cell stacks.

Teach some cells by recurrence without names.

Test novel examples.

Test one-axis neighbors.

Test translation.

Test cross-domain transfer.

Test coordinate-to-example generation.

Test example-to-coordinate recovery.

Record convergence and divergence.

Where humans and GPTs agree, ask what structure supports the agreement.

Where they diverge, ask what each system imported.

Where both systematically confuse neighboring cells, inspect the grammar itself.

Where both generalize across radical carrier changes, examine the invariant.

The aim is not to force either intelligence to vindicate the theory.

The aim is to use controlled structure to make the reconstruction of meaning observable.

---

## 11. The ring closes again

The philosophy began with relation.

The grammar articulated recurrent forms of relational completion.

The cells made those forms generative.

The corpus made them stackable.

Stacking made them teachable.

Teaching makes structural recognition experimentally observable.

Human and GPT reconstruction makes carrier invariance testable.

And carrier invariance returns us to the beginning:

> **intelligibility is relation capable of surviving transformation because enough of its structure can be reconstructed.**

Meaning rides on structure.

Structure becomes available through carriers.

Carriers change.

Intelligibility can persist.

And another intelligence can make it live again.
