# Cell Teaching Template

## A reusable page form for the 54

This template turns each coordinate into a teaching experience rather than a catalogue entry.

The order matters.

The learner should encounter the completed form before the coordinate name.

---

# Cell [NN]

## Blind stack

Show five to twelve examples from semantically distant domains.

Do not reveal the coordinate.

Do not reuse obvious vocabulary across all examples.

Prefer a mixture of:

- ordinary conversation;
- law or agreement;
- mathematics or logic;
- engineering or repair;
- institutions;
- stories;
- spatial or navigational situations;
- scientific practice;
- software or systems;
- childhood or everyday action.

## Recognition

Ask only:

> **What keeps happening?**

The reader should be able to answer in ordinary language.

Do not grade terminology yet.

## Plain invariant

State the completed form in one sentence without coordinate terminology.

> **[Plain invariant]**

This should name the landing the learner has already begun to feel.

---

## Coordinate reveal

> **[Transformation] × [Topology] × [Persistence]**

Then translate each factor into the actual event rather than reciting dictionary definitions.

**What changed?**  
[Transformation as realized in this cell.]

**What made it enough?**  
[Topology as realized in this cell.]

**How does the result remain available?**  
[Persistence as realized in this cell.]

The three answers must describe one event.

---

## Unity sentence

Compress the conjunction:

> **[One sentence showing the Transformation-through-this-Topology-in-this-Persistence.]**

This is the cell-level constraint.

---

## Philosophical disclosure

Answer:

> **What does this cell reveal about intelligibility itself?**

Use three to eight sentences.

The disclosure should connect the cell back to some combination of:

- relation;
- constraint;
- governance;
- sufficiency;
- completion;
- persistence;
- reconstruction;
- inheritance;
- agency in the structural sense.

Do not force every concept into every cell.

Let the cell disclose what it actually makes visible.

Then reveal the neighboring coordinate.

---

## Generation exercise

Give only the coordinate.

Ask the learner to create a new example from a domain not already used on the page.

Success means the new example preserves the cell while changing its semantic surface.

---

## Carrier exercise

Take one example and change carrier:

- prose to diagram;
- speech to writing;
- ordinary language to notation;
- story to rule;
- policy to workflow;
- sentence to visual arrangement.

Ask:

> **What must survive for this still to be the same completed intelligibility?**

This connects cell learning directly to the larger philosophy of structural persistence across carriers.

---

## Meaning exercise

Give two expressions that differ sharply in wording but instantiate the same cell.

Then give two expressions with similar wording that instantiate neighboring cells.

Ask:

> **Which pair is closer in completed meaning, and why?**

This turns the cell into an instrument for studying how meaning rides on structure.

---

## Final return

End every cell with the ring:

> **The words changed. The situation changed. The carrier may change. The completed relational form is what the learner has learned to recognize.**

Then provide links to the three one-axis neighbors most useful for continued discrimination.
